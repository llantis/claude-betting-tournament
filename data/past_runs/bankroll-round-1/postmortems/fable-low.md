## Analysis

**Result:** 23 bets, 9-14, $36.45 staked, -$10.00 (ROI -27%). Peak was $12.47 after day 2; I was out on 2026-10-30, day 9 of 31, the earliest exit of the four players. All four players finished at $0, so simply surviving the round would have won it.

**Where it went wrong**
- **Daily exposure, not pick quality, was the main cause.** I staked 32-72% of my bankroll every day across 2-4 bets, then 100% on the last two days. The rules only require $1 a day.
- **2026-10-27 decided it.** I put $6.00 (63% of a $9.55 bankroll) on three bets and lost all three, dropping to $3.55. That included both totals I ever bet: Under 5.5 NHL for $2.50, my biggest stake of the round, and Under 223.5 NBA for $2.00.
- **2026-10-28 finished it.** I staked $2.55 of $3.55 on two +1.5 lines and lost both, leaving $1.00.
- **"Historical cover rate" claims on +1.5 puck and run lines were stories.** I stated 62-72% confidence on five of them and went 2-3, with nothing in the reasons that verified the numbers.
- **Generic coin-flip reasons lost.** The 50-54% confidence bets went 2-6 for -$6.17; reasons like "near a coin flip, marginal value" and "big spreads price in too much certainty" name no edge.
- **NBA spreads went 2-5 and NHL 3-4**, though both samples are small.

**Calibration**
- Stated confidence averaged 58%, actual win rate was 39%, and prices implied 55%; Brier was 0.262, worse than a coin flip.
- Every confidence band underperformed: 54% stated won 25%, 56% won 44%, 66% won 50%.
- I never showed an edge over the market, so my stated edge of about 3 points was fictional.

**Money management**
- Stakes did not track edge: they ran $1.00-$2.50 on claimed edges of 1-3 points, each 10-25% of bankroll.
- The bigger stakes were not the worse ones: bets of $2 or more went 4-3 for +$0.68, while smaller bets went 5-11.
- The damage came from total daily exposure and from never cutting stakes as the bankroll fell.
- At $1 a day I could not have gone out before day 10 even losing every bet, and at my actual 39% win rate I would have lasted the full 31 days.

**What worked**
- Nothing is proven. Football (NCAAF and NFL) spreads went 3-3 for about break-even, and the first two days went 4-2; six bets each is noise.

# Lessons: fable-low (bankroll)
_Written after round 1 (2026-10-22 to 2026-11-21)._

## Where I went wrong
- Went out on day 9 of 31: 9-14, -$10.00, ROI -27%. All four players finished at $0, so surviving the round would have won it.
- Staked 32-72% of my bankroll every day across 2-4 bets. On 2026-10-27 I put $6.00 (63%) on three bets, lost all three, and fell from $9.55 to $3.55; the next day I staked $2.55 of $3.55 and lost both.
- Overconfident by 19 points: stated 58%, won 39%, prices implied 55%. Every confidence band lost. I showed no edge over the market.
- "Historical cover rate" claims on +1.5 puck and run lines (stated 62-72%) went 2-3. They were unverified stories.
- Totals went 0-2 for -$4.50, including my largest stake ($2.50).
- Generic reasons ("near a coin flip", "big spreads price in too much certainty") at 50-54% confidence went 2-6 for -$6.17.
- NBA spreads went 2-5 and NHL went 3-4.

## What worked
- Nothing is proven. Football spreads went 3-3 for about break-even, and that is noise.
- The arithmetic that would have worked: $1 a day lasts the whole round.

## Rules for my future bets
1. Default day: exactly ONE bet, staked exactly $1.00 (the minimum). No second bet "for diversification".
2. Total daily stake never exceeds 10% of settled bankroll, or $1.00 if that is larger. So a second bet needs a bankroll of $20 or more, and no single stake goes above $1.00 until the bankroll is $15 or more (then at most 8% of bankroll).
3. Never raise stakes after losses, and never stake more because the bankroll is small. At $1-2, still bet $1 on the single best option.
4. Confidence equals the vig-free market probability for that side (my price's implied probability divided by the sum of both sides' implied probabilities), plus at most 2 points, and only if I can name a specific, verified fact from today (confirmed injury or lineup, goalie or starter, rest) that the line has not absorbed. Otherwise state the vig-free probability itself.
5. Never state confidence above 60% unless the price is 1.60 or lower.
6. Never cite a "historical cover rate" or trend percentage I have not looked up today.
7. No totals. No +1.5 puck or run lines justified by base rates. No NBA spreads without a confirmed lineup or injury edge.
8. Pick the one bet with the best-supported reason; if nothing stands out, take the best price available against the market consensus (line shopping within the slate) on a football or standard spread, at $1.
9. Prefer prices of 1.70-2.00. No moneyline underdogs above 2.20.
10. Before submitting, check: one bet, $1, confidence no more than 2 points above the vig-free probability. If any of these fails, fix it.
