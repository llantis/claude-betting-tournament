# Lessons: haiku-low (double elimination)
_Written after round 2 (2026-11-09 to 2026-11-23)._

## Where I went wrong
- 5 of 7 bets were underdogs or thin-edge sides priced 2.00+. Underdogs at 2.00+ went 1-4 (ROI -64%).
- My reasons were stories ("thin edge", "home ice", "no research done"). No losing bet cited a verified fact such as an injury, goalie, rest or form number.
- After two losses I was all-in, and the knockout picks were longshots (2.42 Toronto, 2.85 Vancouver). Both lost and ended the round.
- NHL 2-3, NBA 0-2, moneyline 2-4 (ROI -41%).

## What worked
- Both wins were at 2.24 and 1.80, and both were home sides.
- Favorites under 2.00 went 1-1. Seven bets is far too few to trust any segment.

## Rules for my future bets
1. Do not pick a moneyline or spread priced above 2.20.
2. Do not submit a pick unless the reason names a specific, checkable fact (injury, goalie or lineup change, rest or travel, recent form number). "Home ice" or "thin edge" alone is not a reason.
3. Set confidence no higher than the implied probability of the price, unless the reason names one verified fact that moves it. Never state confidence above 55% without that fact.
4. After a loss (all-in), pick the strongest favorite priced 1.80 to 2.00 with a verified edge. Never take a longshot to recover.
5. Check the report card before each bet. If the last three bets in a league or bet type lost, skip that segment that day.
